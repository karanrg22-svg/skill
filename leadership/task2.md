# Task 2: Summative Plan

## Overview

I will argue that leadership and management overlap more than the classic distinction allows, and test this through Satya Nadella (AC1) and Indra Nooyi (AC3), using transformational leadership and intersectionality.

## AC1: Understanding Leadership

Zaleznik (1977) and Kotter (1990) separate leaders (vision, change) from managers (stability, complexity), which frames the debate. My working position is that leadership and management are distinct functions that one person often performs, so definitions must allow for overlap. Mintzberg (1973) complicates it: observed managers performed interpersonal and informational roles, so overlap is the norm. I will use Bass (1985) on transformational leadership because it connects directly to AC3. Alvesson and Spicer (2012) challenge taken-for-granted assumptions in leadership studies, a check against treating either distinction as settled. I chose this spine because the classic distinction frames the debate, Mintzberg tests it, and transformational theory supplies the lens for AC3.

Nadella at Microsoft tests whether a transformational culture shift also depended on management systems. Choosing an example that may complicate the theory is deliberate. His own account (Nadella, 2017) is leader-authored, so I will triangulate with independent academic and business-press sources [YOU SUPPLY: two sources].

## AC3: Diverse Leader

I chose Nooyi, former PepsiCo chief executive, against three criteria: documented leadership philosophy (Nooyi, 2021); an intersectional position (gender, ethnicity, migration); and a record containing both achievement and criticism, which permits evaluation, not admiration. Her memoir is self-authored, so I will read it as advocacy as well as evidence. I rejected Nadella here to avoid overlap, and because gender does not apply to him.

Crenshaw's (1989) intersectionality, with Rosette et al. (2016) on combined race and gender effects, will show how overlapping identities shaped her context; Bass (1985) shows what she did. I will examine speeches, strategic decisions and stakeholder outcomes, treating decisions that contradict a transformational reading as counter-evidence. Limits: intersectionality is hard to apply to one case and invites over-attribution; evidence is retrospective and media-filtered; transformational theory is leader-centred. I will analyse decisions in context, not Nooyi as a category representative.

## Integration and risks

Both parts test one claim: if leadership and management overlap, transformational success should rely on management systems, and identity should shape how leadership is recognised. The main risk is hero-narrative bias from self-authored sources; I will triangulate with independent analyses [YOU SUPPLY: two sources on Nooyi]. A second is identity reductionism, mitigated by focusing on decisions and outcomes. Evidence that Nooyi's identity changed no decisions, or that outcomes owed more to systems than to her, would lead me to qualify the lens.

The sources disagree: Kotter separates leadership from management, whereas Mintzberg's empirical work finds leadership embedded in managerial work. Much of the evidence is dated, so I will weigh classic practitioner arguments against recent peer-reviewed critique.
