**Autonomy levels (author's adaptation of levels-of-automation research, Parasuraman, Sheridan & Wickens, 2000):**
- **L0 Human-led:** AI used only as a reference tool (glossary lookup, QA checks).
- **L1 AI suggests:** AI proposes options; human decides and produces.
- **L2 AI drafts, human edits:** post-editing with full human QA.
- **L3 AI produces, human audits:** sampled review with escalation rules.
- **L4 (fully autonomous) — not proposed;** stated as a **red line** for creative/performance content.

- **Layout:** left Fig 4 break-even chart; right three benefit cards + evidence tags.

With *m* = 10 (illustrative): e = 0.3 → 40; 0.5 → 60; 0.7 → 80; **0.9 → 100 (break-even)**; 1.0 → 110. Break-even e* = (100 − m)/100. Second line: m = 20 → break-even at e = 0.8. Caption "Figure 4: Illustrative cost-index sensitivity (Source: Author's model; effort ratios to be measured in pilot)". Message: "the advantage disappears if post-editing takes ~80–90% of from-scratch effort".
- **Benefit cards:** (1) **Coverage:** library/long tail become economical (Anderson, 2006) · (2) **Speed:** time-to-availability and day-and-date (Stalk, 1988) · (3) **Quality capacity:** human talent redeployed to adaptation and QA on high-value work.

## 6. RECOMMENDATION CARDS

### R1 — ACTIVATE: library and long-tail localisation (M1–21)
- **What:** apply AI-assisted subtitling (L2), metadata/synopses (L2→L3) and accessibility captions to **back-catalogue titles in underserved languages**, with franchise glossaries, blind native review and MQM scoring; start with 3 languages, expand by gate.

- **Evidence:** Anderson (2006); Stalk (1988); Dell'Acqua et al. (2023) `[V]`; Brynjolfsson et al. (2023) `[V]`; ISO 18587 `[V]`.
- **KPIs:** **Coverage** (% title × language pairs available); **time-to-availability** (days); **MQM score** per 1,000 words by language tier (non-inferior to human baseline within agreed margin); **post-edit effort ratio** (PE time ÷ from-scratch time; break-even e*); **cost index** per content-hour-language (human = 100); **incremental viewing hours** of newly localised titles vs matched control `[V]`; complaint rate per 10k hours.
- **Gate (promotion):** MQM non-inferior on blind evaluation + complaint rate within bound + talent consultation signed + legal clearance for two consecutive quarters. **Demotion:** automatic if MQM or complaints breach thresholds.
- **Risks:** low-resource language quality → human review first; viewer rejection → opt-out/quality feedback loop.
- **Why not alternative:** status-quo vendor-only keeps the long tail dark; full automation across tiers risks authenticity.

### R2 — AUGMENT: mid-tier dubbing with consent (M6–21)
- **What:** use AI for **dubbing script adaptation, timing and lip-sync assistance (L2)** with human dubbing directors and human voice performers; **synthetic voices only for narrow, opt-in, compensated use-cases** under the **Consent, Credit & Compensation Charter** (independent guild representation); no replacement of lead performances.
- **Owner:** Head of Content Operations + Head of Talent Relations; Legal.
- **Evidence:** Parasuraman, Sheridan & Wickens (2000); Chaume (2012); SAG-AFTRA/Equity positions `[V]`; EU AI Act transparency `[V]`; Chesney & Citron (2019).
- **KPIs:** consent coverage (100% of synthetic voice assets); red-line breaches = 0; MQM and audience-acceptance score (viewer ratings of dubbing quality, complaint rate); cost index; director/performer satisfaction pulse.
- **Gate:** charter ratified (G1); pilot in two markets with contrasting dubbing cultures (G2); non-inferior audience acceptance and zero breaches (G3).
- **Kill-switch:** any consent breach pauses the voice pipeline.
- **Risks:** guild dispute → early engagement; deepfake misuse → provenance and access controls.
- **Why not alternative:** blanket synthetic dubbing is legally and reputationally fragile.

### R3 — PROTECT: craft guarantee and talent partnership (M1 onward)
- **What:** **Craft Guarantee**: flagship and children's content remain human-led (L0–L1) by policy; **Quality & Ethics Council** with linguists, directors, guild observers; **AI-Linguist Academy**; role redesign (franchise language leads, quality leads); transition pledges; **gain-sharing** of verified productivity (e.g., funding upskilling and rates).
- **Owner:** Chief Creative Officer + CHRO (accountable); Quality & Ethics Council.
- **Evidence:** Hackman & Oldham (1976); Petriglieri (2011); Rousseau (1989); Schuler & Namioka (1993); Bridges (2009); Acemoglu & Restrepo (2019).
- **KPIs:** flagship/kids L0–L1 compliance = 100%; retention of in-house and core vendor linguists; academy tier completion; TRI pulse shift; job-characteristics scores; vendor participation; council recommendations adopted or answered with rationale.
- **Gate:** at M9 and M15 — retention and satisfaction not worse than baseline; otherwise pause tier promotion.
- **Risks:** guarantee seen as token → publish audit; productivity gains not shared → CFO-owned rule.
- **Why not alternative:** pure efficiency programme triggers identity threat and quality erosion.
