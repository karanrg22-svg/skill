## 4. PER-FRAMING ASSESSMENT

### A — Story-led flywheel (ML personalisation; creative teams)
- **What it argues:** personalisation only creates *sustained* advantage if governed with creatives and aimed at franchise-ecosystem value and discovery breadth, not watch-time.
- **Why a harsh marker likes it:** mirrors the brief; clear golden thread; quantified (illustrative) benefit with honest caveats; strong critique habits (source strength, Kotter critique, "resistance as information"); clear recommendations with gates and KPIs.
- **Main risks:** can look "safe/standard" if originality isn't surfaced; the churn model is an illustration (must be labelled); evidence from Netflix/McKinsey is self-reported/consultancy (must be treated as upper bound).
- **Hardest-to-verify items:** Disney subscriber/ARPU/churn inputs; Antenna churn data; McKinsey personalisation figures; WGA 2023 terms; Disney's reporting changes.
- **What a harsh marker would attack:** "advantage claim without conditions" (mitigated by VRIN/parity argument); "creative veto = bottleneck" (mitigated by SLAs/delegation); "numbers are made up" (mitigated by labelling/sensitivity).
- **Best for you if:** you want the safest route to 80+ and the cleanest viva.

### B — Earn it, show it, keep it (PETs; parents)
- **What it argues:** in a market of similar catalogues and algorithms, verified family trust is a defensible differentiator; achieve it through privacy-by-architecture, show it through participation and independent audit, keep it through incentives and capability.
- **Why a harsh marker likes it:** distinctive, ethics-integrated, honest about the privacy–utility trade-off and the privacy paradox, and explicit that exposure figures are *ceilings not forecasts*.
- **Main risks:** technical depth (federated learning, differential privacy) must be correct; evidence that families pay or stay for privacy is mixed; "customers" as AC2 stakeholder needs careful internal-impact coverage (Star model/culture) to avoid looking like a marketing essay.
- **Hardest-to-verify items:** Pew/Cisco/IBM figures; regulator fines (ICO, Irish DPC); any FTC action involving Disney; Disney revenue; kids' profile ad policy; age-of-consent thresholds by jurisdiction.
- **What a harsh marker would attack:** "privacy-washing"; "no revenue case" (mitigated by avoided-loss logic and pilot measurement); "competitors can copy" (mitigated by verification and moving-baseline argument).
- **Best for you if:** you want a distinctive, ethics-forward argument and are comfortable with law/tech.

### C — One Disney platform (unified cloud data platform; ops & compliance)
- **What it argues:** unification is an organisational redesign enabled by technology; unify the identity/billing/data spine, differentiate the experience; change structure, decision rights and culture alongside architecture.
- **Why a harsh marker likes it:** strongest mutual-influence analysis (Conway, decision rights, Westrum), concrete engineering/operations metrics (DORA, SLOs), explicit wave plan with parity gates and rollback, fair-process treatment of the workforce.
- **Main risks:** least public evidence on Disney's real technical estate; can read as dry or IT-centric; benefits mostly operational unless linked to strategy (handled via digital options/economies of scope); FMEA scores are subjective.
- **Hardest-to-verify items:** Disney's 2025–26 product consolidation timeline; outage history; DORA benchmark bands; data-protection/transfer law by market.
- **What a harsh marker would attack:** "this is just IT migration" (mitigated by the org-redesign thesis); "no advantage as rivals use cloud" (mitigated by scope/option logic); "persona is made up" (mitigated by labelling).
- **Best for you if:** you want the strongest LO2 analysis and have an IT/operations background.

### D — Scale the pipeline, protect the craft (GenAI localisation; localisation teams)
- **What it argues:** GenAI creates sustained advantage in localisation only where autonomy is earned tier by tier; scale the library/long tail, augment mid-tier dubbing with consent, protect flagship and children's content.
- **Why a harsh marker likes it:** original, ethically serious, operationally concrete (autonomy ladder, promotion/demotion rules, red lines), explicit about evidence limits.
- **Main risks:** thinnest and most transferable evidence; fast-moving law and labour agreements; Disney's own AI stance unknown; accuracy of AI-dubbing claims; harder viva.
- **Hardest-to-verify items:** SAG-AFTRA/Equity terms; EU AI Act dates; copyright guidance; Disney litigation/partnerships; AI-dubbing pilots at rivals; viewer acceptance data.
- **What a harsh marker would attack:** "evidence is from other tasks" (mitigated by explicit caveat); "ethics is a list" (mitigated by red lines and gates); "unverified legal claims" (must be verified or removed).
- **Best for you if:** you have time to verify and enjoy defending GenAI/labour issues.

## 6. WHAT DETERMINES 80+ IN ANY FRAMING (common success factors)
1. **Critical evaluation, not description**: counter-argument + rebuttal on every major claim; critique each theory in a line.
2. **Source integration**: ≥2 academic + ≥2 industry/regulatory sources per key claim; conflicts stated; vendor/consultancy/self-reported sources flagged as such.
3. **Disney-specific application** using the brief and your in-class notes.
4. **Quantification with honesty**: illustrative models clearly labelled; sensitivity ranges; no invented dollar figures.
5. **Recommendations that a leadership team could act on**: owner, KPI, timeline, gate, risk, cost shape, fallback.
6. **Integrated ethics/strategy**: ethics shown as a design constraint with admitted costs.
7. **Presentation quality**: ≤65 words per slide, numbered/sourced figures, notes that expand rather than script.
8. **Referencing precision**: two-way citation check; Harvard consistency; every `[V]` resolved.
9. **Video discipline**: 9:15–9:45, signposted, not read.
10. **Admin**: cover sheet complete; SRN only; AI use declared and prompts retained; word count stated; both submissions early.
