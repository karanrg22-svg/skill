## 2. THE ARGUMENT (lock before opening PowerPoint)
| Element | Locked choice |
|---|---|
| **Technology (one)** | **Privacy-enhancing technologies (PETs) for personalisation**: on-device inference + federated learning + differential privacy, so recommendations are produced **without centralising raw viewing data**, starting with kids' and family profiles |
| **Problem** | Disney+ needs data to personalise and to monetise (ad tier, global expansion), but its core customers are **families**, regulators are tightening on children's data, **consent-based privacy is failing in practice**, and compliance is fragmented by region; internal views differ (operations/compliance vs data/marketing — from the brief) |
| **Stakeholder (one)** | **Parents and guardians** (customers and the legal consent-givers for children) |
| **Thesis (S1/S2 and S11)** | In a market of near-identical catalogues and algorithms, **verifiable family trust** is a defensible differentiator; build it through **architecture over policy**, prove it through **independent verification and parent participation**, and sustain it because **trust is a moving baseline** |
| **Spine** | **EARN** (R1) → **SHOW** (R2) → **KEEP** (R3) |
| **Three recommendations** | **R1** Privacy-by-Architecture Standard + PET pilot on kids' profiles · **R2** Family Trust Layer (Family Data Passport, Parent Advisory Panel, independent audit, published scorecard) · **R3** Trust & Privacy Engineering operating model (guild, release gates, aligned rewards, capability) |
| **Thread phrases** | "architecture over policy" · "verify, don't promise" · "trust is a moving baseline" |

**Five critical moves to land (these separate 70s from 80s):**
1. **Policy and consent can't fix this** — the privacy paradox (people worry but click through) means protection must not depend on a click.
2. **Make the privacy–utility trade-off explicit**, and test the tolerance in a pilot.
3. **Benefits are avoided loss and option value, not a promised revenue uplift**; exposure figures are *ceilings, not forecasts*.
4. **Verify, don't promise**: independent audit and publishing failures guard against "privacy-washing".
5. **Concede that trust may become table stakes** (the regulatory floor rises), so advantage comes from staying ahead and proving it.
