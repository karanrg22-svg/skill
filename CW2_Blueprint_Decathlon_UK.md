## 2. THE GOLDEN THREAD (the spine of the report)

Harsh markers reward **coherence** and penalise four separate mini-essays. Decide this chain **once the data is in**, then force every section to refer to it.

```
Context (UK sports retail, digital behaviour)
   → ONE persona → ONE evidence-led insight ("the gap")
   → TWO SMART objectives that close the gap (linked: O1 feeds O2)
   → SOSTAC strategy (STP + tactics) built on the insight
   → 4Ps that deliver the persona's needs and move the KPIs
   → Control: KPIs that map back to O1/O2
```

If a paragraph ends with description, rewrite it.

### 4.3 TASK 1: Market and Customer Insight (960)

**1.1 Digital presence and effectiveness (300) → LO1, LO3**
- **Method:** a *channel effectiveness audit* using the owned/paid/earned classification and the RACE funnel (Smith; Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022). **Figure 1** is a scorecard: Channel | Role in journey | Evidence `[D]` | Benchmark (Sports Direct / JD / Amazon / brand direct-to-consumer) | Verdict (Strong/Adequate/Weak).
- **Channels to audit:** website; mobile app; email/CRM; paid search and social; organic social (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook); marketplace/third-party; reviews (Trustpilot, app stores).
- **Evidence ideas (all free):** Similarweb traffic and channel mix; app-store ratings and review counts with theme coding of recent reviews; Trustpilot score and complaint themes; Meta Ad Library and TikTok Creative Center for rivals' ad activity; social following and engagement rate (calculated, state the formula); Google Trends for share of search; Companies House accounts for scale and direction `[D1–D6]`.
- **Critical moves:** (1) state data limitations (public metrics are proxies, not conversion data); (2) judge effectiveness against a benchmark, not in isolation; (3) end with a ranked verdict ("strongest in X, weakest in Y").
- **Value creation:** link each strong channel to a customer benefit and a business metric (e.g., click-and-collect → convenience and lower delivery cost).
- **Avoid:** listing channels with follower counts and no judgement.

**1.2 Market environment, PESTLE prioritised (220) → LO3**
- Use PESTLE to *structure*, but **only report the six most consequential factors**. Rate each for impact and certainty (**Figure 2**, a 2×2 or table with Harvey balls). Then one "so what" line each.
- **Candidate factors to test (check currency):**

| Factor | Candidate | Source family |
|---|---|---|
| Political/Legal | UK GDPR and PECR; Data (Use and Access) Act 2025; DMCC Act 2024 (drip pricing, fake reviews); ASA influencer rules | ICO, legislation.gov.uk, ASA/CMA |
| Economic | Cost-of-living pressure and consumer confidence; the value-seeking shift | GfK, ONS |
| Social | Participation trends in running, gym and cycling; health consciousness | Sport England Active Lives |

| Environmental | Circular economy, resale/repair, green-claims scrutiny | CMA Green Claims Code, Decathlon sustainability reporting |

- **Critique line (one sentence):** PESTLE is descriptive and static (Aguilar, 1967 as origin), so it is prioritised here and read together with the competitor audit.

**1.3 Persona and customer journey (290) → LO3, LO1**
- Persona card: demographics `[D]`, needs framed as jobs-to-be-done (Christensen et al., 2016), preferences, device and platform behaviour, barriers.
- **Journey:** Kotler, Kartajaya and Setiawan's (2017) 5A path (Aware → Appeal → Ask → Act → Advocate) mapped against current Decathlon touchpoints. Mark **moments of friction and opportunity** (e.g., "Ask": sizing and fit uncertainty; "Act": delivery cost). Reference Lemon and Verhoef (2016) on journey stages and Verhoef, Kannan and Inman (2015) on omnichannel behaviour (research online, purchase in store).
- **Critique line:** a single persona simplifies a diverse market, so it is triangulated across at least three data sources (Appendix E).
- **Avoid:** a stock-photo persona with hobbies and a favourite colour. Every attribute must change a marketing decision.

**1.4 Value creation and the insight (150) → LO1**
- Two-sided value statement for the persona's journey: customer value (convenience, confidence, price) and business value (conversion, CAC, CLV). Use Vargo and Lusch (2004) (value co-created through the customer experience) and Porter and Kramer (2011) only if the link is genuine.
- **Evaluate:** which value mechanism is working and which is leaking, with the evidence.
- End with the **insight statement** in one boxed sentence. Everything after it must trace back to it.

### 4.4 TASK 2: Strategic Marketing Objectives (830)

**2.1–2.2 Two SMART objectives (340) → LO2, LO1**
- **Figure 4** (table) shows both objectives with S, M, A, R, T in columns. The text justifies, it does not repeat the table.
- **Specific and measurable:** one metric each, one definition, one data source (e.g., "monthly active app users aged 25–34, Firebase/GA4").
- **Achievable (the part markers look for):** justify the target with a *benchmark* (an industry growth rate `[D]`) or Locke and Latham's (2002) goal-setting finding (stretching but attainable goals improve performance). Show the baseline-to-target logic in one sentence.
- **Relevant:** tie to the persona and to organisational goals (growth, loyalty, performance).
- **Time-bound:** 12 months with 3-month checkpoints.
- **Customer and organisation value:** one explicit sentence for each of customer and organisation per objective.
- Doran (1981) is the original SMART source. Note that SMART can reward vanity metrics (Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022), so each objective ties to a business outcome.

**2.3 Dual value logic (90) → LO1**
- Explain how O1 creates engagement value (relevance, guidance) and O2 creates loyalty value (rewards, personalisation), and how value then flows to the business (lifetime value, lower acquisition cost). Evaluate the weaker link (engagement does not automatically become repeat purchase).

**2.4 Two key challenges (270) → LO2**
- **C1: Competitive and price-perception pressure** in a saturated UK sports retail market. It raises acquisition costs and weakens conversion (affects O1 and O2).
- **C2: Data privacy and measurement limits** (UK GDPR/PECR consent, platform tracking limits). They shrink addressable audiences, weaken personalisation and complicate attribution (affects O2 most).
- Frame them through a **mini-SWOT/TOWS** (Weihrich, 1982) *derived from* Task 1's evidence, and not repeated from it. Score likelihood × impact (**Figure 5**), state the effect on a named KPI, and give a mitigation per challenge (e.g., first-party data strategy, value-based membership).
- **Critique line:** SWOT lists are subjective (Hill and Westbrook, 1997), so the scoring uses data from Task 1.

**2.5 Stress-test (130) → LO2**
- Where might the objectives conflict (O1's growth from discounts versus O2's margin)? What is the weakest assumption? What would trigger a revision? This is the section that gets 80+ marks for "evaluating plan effectiveness", so do not skip it.

## 6. CRITICAL-THINKING TOOLKIT (what lifts a 70 into an 85)

1. **Triangulate:** every major claim has two or more independent sources, or a source plus your own audit evidence.
2. **Benchmark:** never say "good". Say "better/worse than [rival/standard]" with a number and a date.
3. **Evaluate every framework once:** one sentence on its limit, then show how you compensate (see the "critique lines" above). Do not write essays about the theory.
4. **Show alternatives rejected:** at least three decisions in the report show "Option A versus B, chosen because…" (targeting, pricing, influencer scale).
5. **State assumptions and limitations plainly:** public data, indicative budgets, single persona, UK only.
6. **Quantify where possible,** but not beyond what the evidence supports. The brief says no complex forecasting.
7. **UK-specific context:** UK GDPR/PECR, ASA, DMCC Act, Ofcom data, seasonal UK events. Generic global statistics lose marks.
8. **"So what" test:** after every paragraph ask, *so what for the persona, the objective or the decision?* If there is no answer, cut or rewrite.
9. **Close the loop:** the Control stage ties back to the Task 2 objectives, and the Conclusion ties back to the Introduction's roadmap.
10. **Practical realism:** feasibility, owners and sequencing show that you understand implementation. Senior management would ask these questions.

### 6.1 Evaluative sentence stems (use varied wording, do not overuse any one)
- "This suggests… however, the evidence is limited by…"
- "Compared with [rival], [metric] indicates…, which implies…"
- "The strongest justification for… is…, although…"
- "This option was rejected because… despite…"
- "Consequently, the plan prioritises… ahead of…"

### 6.2 Writing style
- Third person, formal, UK spelling, no contractions, abbreviations defined at first use.
- Short sentences, active voice where possible, one idea per sentence.
- Headings mirror the brief's wording so the marker can match each criterion.
- No filler ("in today's fast-paced digital world"), no repeating the brief, no padding definitions.
