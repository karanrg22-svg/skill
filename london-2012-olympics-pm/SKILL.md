# London 2012 Olympics — Applied Project Management CW5

Blueprint for a General Project Management / Construction-lens essay analysing
the London 2012 Olympics as a megaproject, structured against a five-AC marking
rubric. Use this skill to draft new sections, expand existing ones, or review a
draft against the rubric before submission.

## 0. How a harsh marker actually reads this (rubric read backwards)

Comparing the marking guide's 80-100% column against its 0-39% column across
all five ACs, the same five factors decide the grade every time:

| Marker rewards (chase this) | Marker penalizes (kill this) |
| --- | --- |
| Each concept mapped to a specific, dated London 2012 event or decision, not described in the abstract | "The project had risks such as X, Y, Z" with no case evidence attached |
| 2+ sources per section, integrated mid-argument, not dumped at the end of a paragraph | 1 source per section, or sources cited but not actually used to make a point |
| Critical evaluation — "this worked because... but was limited because..." | Descriptive/imitative writing ("The ODA managed the project well") |
| Tools applied with real numbers (budget, m³, dates, £) from the case, not generic templates | Vague, unrealistic, or unsupported figures |
| Visuals (Gantt, WBS, risk matrix, cost breakdown, S-curve) referenced and interpreted in the text | Visuals absent, or present but "lacking clarity" / not discussed |
| A recommendation/reflection layer in every section | Stopping at "what happened" without "what it means for PM practice" |

Before finalizing any section, check it against this table explicitly.

## 1. Document architecture (order + formatting)

1. Cover page (BPP cover sheet)
2. Title page
3. Table of Contents (auto-generated, page numbers)
4. Introduction — 150w
5. AC1 Project Life Cycle Analysis — 650w
6. AC2 Business Case, Scope & Schedule — 650w
7. AC3 Resource Planning & Budget Analysis — 650w
8. AC4 Risk Assessment & Contingency Planning — 650w
9. AC5 Progress Review & Intervention Strategies — 650w
10. Conclusion & Reflection — 100w
11. References (Harvard, alphabetical)
12. Appendices (extra tables/calculations that would break the flow of the main text)

Formatting: Arial/Calibri 11, 1.5 spacing, page numbers, numbered headings
(1.0, 1.1, 1.2...), consistent Heading 1/2 styles — formatting reads as a proxy
for "professional/practical skills," which is its own graded row.

## 2. AC1 — Project Life Cycle Analysis (650 words)

**Word split:** ~80w life-cycle model identification/thesis · ~400w six
stages mapped to real ODA/CLM events · ~120w critical evaluation of what a
more structured model would've changed · ~50w link sentence into AC2.

**Theory to deploy:** APM Body of Knowledge (7th ed.) six-stage life cycle
(business case prep, planning, design, development, deployment, evaluation);
Munns and Bjeirmi (1996) on life cycle vs project success; Maylor (2010) on
waterfall vs agile/hybrid; Shenhar and Dvir's (2007) NTCP diamond for
justifying why a hybrid model suited a fixed-deadline megaproject.

**Case facts to hook each stage to:** ODA established 2006
(business case/initiation); 2-year planning phase; 220 buildings demolished,
2.3m m³ excavated (design/development evidence); four-year build to 2011 test
events (development/deployment); one-year testing culminating July 2012
(evaluation); £8.77bn budget as the governance anchor across all stages
(NAO, 2012).

**Marker-pleasing move:** explicitly argue the programme ran a hybrid model —
waterfall at the macro/governance level (immovable 2012 deadline, fixed
stages) but with fast-track/concurrent engineering at the construction level —
then critique what a more disciplined stage-gate process would have added
(this is exactly the "alternative approaches and their potential impact" line
the 80-100% band asks for).

**Visual:** a life-cycle Gantt/timeline graphic (2006-2012) with stage
boundaries labelled — put it here, not in an appendix, so the marker sees it
while reading the argument.

**Avoid:** listing the six stages as definitions only — verbatim the 40-49%
band ("all six stages are identified with basic definitions").

## 3. AC2 — Business Case, Scope & Schedule (650 words)

**Word split:** ~150w business case (tourism/competitiveness,
sustainability/renewable energy, national identity/international
positioning) · ~200w scope statement (deliverables/exclusions/
assumptions/constraints, ideally as a table) · ~200w schedule
(milestones/dependencies, Gantt-referenced) · ~100w critical link: how the
business case drove scope and schedule decisions.

**Theory to deploy:** Business case rationale via cost-benefit analysis logic
(Maylor, 2010; APM BoK); Atkinson (1999) on the iron triangle and why
fixed-deadline megaprojects distort the cost/time/quality trade-off; Miller
and Lessard (2001) on front-end shaping of large engineering projects; PMI
PMBOK on scope statements/WBS logic.

**Case facts:** 2.5km² Stratford brownfield regeneration; 90% material reuse
target (sustainability pillar); Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park post-Games
legacy (DCMS, 2013); £14.2bn trade/investment impact 2012-14
(UK Government, 2013) as benefits-realisation evidence for the original
business case.

**Marker-pleasing move:** build an actual scope statement table
(Deliverables / Exclusions / Assumptions / Constraints) — the rubric
explicitly separates "scope statement includes key elements" (mid band) from
"exemplary in clarity and completeness" (top band). Pair with a milestone
schedule table or Gantt (2006 ODA formed → 2007 masterplan → 2008-11
construction → 2011 test events → 2012 Games) citing explicit dependencies
(e.g., transport infrastructure preceding venue completion).

**Avoid:** treating "business case" as a synonym for "why the Olympics is
good" — the fail band is literally "no clear business case rationale."

## 4. AC3 — Resource Planning & Budget Analysis (650 words)

**Word split:** ~150w resource categories (human/materials/equipment/
financial/technical) · ~250w resource allocation plan/table with quantities
& sequencing · ~150w budget build (bottom-up or parametric estimate
reconciled to £8.77bn) · ~100w how constraints affected schedule/risk/
contractor performance.

**Theory to deploy:** Estimation methods — bottom-up, analogous, parametric
(PMI PMBOK; Kerzner, 2017); resource histograms/levelling (Maylor, 2010);
Winch (2010) *Managing Construction Projects* for construction-specific
labour/material/plant categorisation; digital-twin/BIM use in megaprojects
(Davies and Mackenzie, 2014) for the "technical resources" category.

**Case facts:** 220 buildings demolished, 2.3m m³ excavation, 30 new
bridges, 90% material reuse — turn into a resource allocation/quantity table
with clearly-stated assumed unit rates, reconciled to the real £8.77bn
confirmed budget (NAO, 2012) so the bottom-up estimate is anchored to a
verifiable figure.

**Marker-pleasing move:** a resource-based budget table broken into
labour/materials/equipment/contingency/overheads, with a short narrative on
cost variation risk (skilled labour scarcity, steel price volatility
pre-2008 financial crisis — a genuinely citable real-world pressure on the
2008-2012 programme). This is the single highest-differentiating AC — the
top band wants "strategic, optimised" resource decisions "aligned with
constraints," which only shows through arithmetic, not description.

**Visual:** cost breakdown structure (pie or stacked bar) + a resource
histogram for peak labour demand during construction (2008-2011).

**Avoid:** listing resource types with no numbers attached — explicitly the
20-39% band ("budget calculations are vague, unrealistic, or incomplete").

## 5. AC4 — Risk Assessment & Contingency Planning (650 words)

**Word split:** ~150w risk identification/categorisation (5 categories from
the brief) · ~200w risk matrix (probability x impact, ratings justified) ·
~200w evaluation of actual risk responses used · ~100w proposed contingency
improvements.

**Theory to deploy:** Hillson (2009) and Chapman and Ward (2003) for risk
identification/response frameworks (avoid/transfer/mitigate/accept);
PRINCE2 (AXELOS) risk register logic; Flyvbjerg, Stewart and Budzier (2016)
Oxford Olympics Study — directly on Olympic cost/schedule overrun risk, the
single most relevant academic source for this AC; Locatelli et al. (2017) on
megaproject governance risk.

**Case facts:** political risk (2008 financial crisis hitting a
2005-bid-era budget), governance risk (multiple stakeholders: ODA, CLM,
LOCOG, government), environmental/sustainability constraints (contaminated
brownfield remediation before build could start), contractor/supply-chain
risk implicit in a 220-building demolition programme, schedule risk from the
immovable opening-ceremony deadline.

**Marker-pleasing move:** a genuine 5x5 risk matrix table with each risk
scored (likelihood/impact) and justified in one sentence each — satisfies
"accurate and justified risk matrix ratings" verbatim. Then evaluate what
actually protected the programme (contingency budget within the £8.77bn,
phased procurement, independent NAO assurance) versus what modern practice
would add (quantitative Monte Carlo contingency modelling, dynamic risk
register reviews) — this "propose improved practice" layer separates 60%
from 80%+.

**Avoid:** risks listed as a bullet list with no matrix and no discussion of
response effectiveness.

## 6. AC5 — Progress Review & Intervention Strategies (650 words)

**Word split:** ~100w overview of why monitoring mattered at this
scale/complexity · ~250w KPIs/tools (SPI, CPI, milestone tracking,
quality/defect metrics, stakeholder indicators) applied to the case · ~200w
intervention points identified · ~100w recommended corrective techniques
(re-baselining, change control, crashing/fast-tracking).

**Theory to deploy:** Earned Value Management — Anbari (2003), Fleming and
Koppelman (2010) — to define and illustratively apply SPI/CPI logic;
Kerzner (2017) on project control techniques; Turner (2014) on intervention
and re-baselining.

**Case facts:** the 2011 test-events year functioning as a real-world
integration/verification checkpoint (a genuine intervention point to
analyse); NAO's periodic assurance reviews as an actual monitoring mechanism
used on the real programme (NAO, 2012) — this lets you apply KPI theory to a
documented, real oversight structure.

**Marker-pleasing move:** construct an illustrative S-curve or
milestone-tracker chart (planned vs actual progress across 2006-2012), use
it to identify a plausible intervention point, then map that to a named
control technique. This "identify deviation → propose specific
intervention" chain is exactly "analysis of missed intervention
opportunities."

**Avoid:** naming KPIs without ever calculating or illustrating them against
case data — explicitly the 40-49% band ("mentions EVM or APM; 1 source
cited").

## 7. Introduction (150w) and Conclusion & Reflection (100w)

**Introduction:** one sentence establishing the case (London 2012 as a
megaproject), one sentence stating your role/lens (retrospective PM
analysis), one sentence previewing the five ACs in order, one sentence
flagging your critical/evaluative stance.

**Conclusion:** synthesise the cross-cutting lesson (how life cycle
discipline, business case clarity, resourcing realism, risk foresight, and
KPI monitoring interacted — not a re-list of each AC), plus one
forward-looking reflection sentence on megaproject practice generally.

## 10. Paragraph-level blueprint (apply to every AC paragraph)

1. **Claim** — one sentence stating the PM point.
2. **Theory** — cite the model/framework that grounds it.
3. **Case evidence** — a specific dated fact/number from London 2012.
4. **Critical evaluation** — what worked, what didn't, why (use
   "however/although/despite" — the rubric's language itself rewards
   critique over description).
5. **So-what/recommendation** — what better practice would change.

## How to use this skill

When asked to draft, expand, or review this assignment:

- Draft one AC at a time, hitting the word split above and running each
  paragraph through the section 10 five-step blueprint.
- Every fact used must trace back to a source in section 8 — do not invent
  figures or citations; if a number isn't in this blueprint or a verified
  source, flag it rather than fabricating it.
- Before calling a section finished, check it against the section 0 table
  (marker rewards vs penalizes) and confirm the relevant visual from
  section 9 is present and interpreted in the text, not just dropped in.
- Keep formatting consistent with section 1 (numbered headings, 1.5
  spacing, Heading 1/2 styles) if producing a full document.
