## 1. Harsh-marker lens: what to include and what to cut

### What a harsh marker loves
1. **Judgement, not summary.** Every paragraph answers "so what?" and takes a position.
2. **Theory used as a tool.** A theory is applied to a specific behaviour or case, then tested for what it explains and what it misses.
3. **At least one critique per major theory**, cited to a source rather than asserted.
4. **Evidence for every claim about yourself**, such as a concrete incident, feedback received or a result, not "I am empathetic".
5. **Honest weakness stated precisely**, with its consequence for others.
6. **A visible change in thinking**: "I used to think X; Week 4 showed Y; therefore I now do Z."
7. **Alignment to the summative.** Task 1 feeds AC4 and Task 2 feeds AC1/AC3, and the brief's own wording appears in your structure.
8. **Precision.** Authors, years and pages are exact. Figures are only used when you have verified them.
9. **A reference list that matches the plan.** Every planned claim has a source, and the mix is academic (journal articles, books) plus credible business sources (HBR, company reports).

### What to remove
- Dictionary definitions ("Leadership is the process of…") with no argument attached.
- Listing theories without saying why *these* ones.
- Generic self-praise or vague phrases such as "good communicator", "passion", "team player".
- Unsupported claims and "studies show" with no citation.
- Padding, signposting essays ("This essay will…" beyond one line) and repeating the brief back.
- Name-dropping a leader without a rationale, or choosing one for fame rather than fit.
- Reference list errors: wrong format, missing years or pages, Wikipedia or blogs, sources never used.
- Word-count games: tiny fonts, cutting articles until it reads badly.
- Hindsight tidiness. Reflection that is all success and no discomfort reads as untrue.

**Content rules**
- Anchor in your **Week 4 activity** (EI, EI-based leadership styles, SI, empathy, relational leadership, trust and empowerment, personal development). Use your *real* score, feedback or scenario, never an invented one.
- Pick **one lens, with one contrasting critique**, and do not list everything. Strong combinations:
  - Goleman's EI competencies, plus a critique (Antonakis, Ashkanasy and Dasborough, 2009).
  - Goleman's leadership styles, with the point that style should *fit the context* rather than being a trait of yours.
- Each claim about yourself should say: **what I did → which concept explains it → what that cost or gained the other person → what I will change.**
- Include **one uncomfortable insight**. For example, your empathy may be strong in rapport but weak in challenging poor performance, which means trust is built without accountability. Use whichever is true for you.
- A bullet or two of action is fine, but each must be measurable (what, by when, how you will know).

#### Section 1, AC1 theories: recommended spine (⚠ read each before citing)
- **Leadership vs management**
  - Zaleznik (1977): managers and leaders differ in personality and orientation.
  - Kotter (1990): management copes with complexity, leadership with change.
  - Mintzberg (1973): the actual content of managerial work is messier than the roles imply.
- **Your critical point:** the dichotomy is contested. Mintzberg shows managers *do* lead, so the clean split is more conceptual than practical. State this in one sentence and say how it shapes your definition.
- **Leadership approaches**
  - Burns (1978) and Bass (1985): transformational vs transactional.
  - One critique (⚠ choose from Alvesson and Spicer, 2012, or a charisma or "heroic leader" critique that you have read).
- **Why this selection:** a mix of classic and critical, and it connects directly to the lens in AC3 and the EI focus in Task 1. A harsh marker will ask "why these?", so answer it in a clause.

#### Section 2, real-world example
- **Recommended: Satya Nadella at Microsoft** (the culture shift toward empathy and a growth mindset, and the leadership vs management contrast).
  - Source options: Nadella's *Hit Refresh* (2017). It is a first-person account, so treat it as potentially self-serving.
  - Add a **critical or independent source** (⚠ find a credible business-press or HBR piece) so you are not relying on the leader's own narrative.
- Using Nadella here means you **do not reuse** your AC3 leader, which avoids overlap and wasted words.
- Alternative if you prefer a failure case: a leadership failure with a documented outcome. It shows range.

#### Section 3, AC3 leader and rationale
- **Recommended: Indra Nooyi** (former CEO, PepsiCo; female, Indian-born, immigrant leader in a majority-male, majority-white executive context).
  - Why she works:
    - There is a clear intersectional story (gender, ethnicity, immigration).
    - She has a published, citable leadership philosophy ("Performance with Purpose").
    - Her record has real strengths and real criticisms (for example the health impact of PepsiCo's products), which gives you something critical to analyse.
  - Source option: Nooyi (2021) *My Life in Full* (⚠ confirm details). Again, a self-account, so pair it with independent sources.
- **Fallback:** Satya Nadella, but only if you did not use him in Section 2. Otherwise choose another leader with documented diversity and a record with criticisms.
- **Rationale must be criterion-based**, for example: documented leadership evidence, credible sources available, diversity dimension relevant to the lens, and enough complexity to evaluate. Add one sentence on why *not* someone more obvious.
- Do **not** assume diversity is the leader's whole story. Say how you will avoid reducing her to her identity.

#### Section 4, theoretical lens
- **Recommended pairing:**
  - **Primary: Intersectionality** (Crenshaw, 1989), and **Rosette et al. (2016)** for race and gender effects in leadership evaluation. Together they explain *how* identity shaped the leader's experience.
  - **Secondary: transformational leadership** (Bass, 1985), to analyse *what* she did. This ties to AC1.
  - Optional: **role congruity** (Eagly and Karau, 2002) for why leaders can face penalties when behaviour doesn't fit stereotypes.
- **State the limits of the lens**, for example:
  - Intersectionality is hard to apply to a single case.
  - Evidence is largely retrospective and media-filtered.
  - Transformational theory has been criticised as leader-centric.
  A harsh marker rewards you for naming these *before* they do.
- Say **how** you will operationalise it: what evidence you will look for (decisions, speeches, stakeholder outcomes) and what would count as evidence against your interpretation.

#### Section 5, integration and risk
- One sentence linking AC1 definitions to the AC3 analysis, so the plan reads as one argument.
- Name **one methodological risk**: self-authored sources and media hero narratives. Say how you will triangulate (autobiography plus independent academic plus press).
