## 6. RECOMMENDATION CARDS (Table 4 expanded; keep S10 to the summary row)

### R1 — Creative–Data Governance Council (M0–6, then standing)
- **What:** cross-functional council (creative leads, data science, product, legal/compliance, children's-content safeguarding) with: **creative veto** over placements for named franchises/slots; **editorial override** with logged rationale; kids' safeguards; model-change review; quarterly ethics/bias audit.
- **Owner:** Chief Creative Officer (chair) + Chief Data Officer; Compliance reports to council.
- **Evidence:** Ford et al. (2008); Floridi et al. (2018); Mittelstadt (2019); ICO code `[V]`.
- **KPIs:** council charter ratified (M2); guardrail KPIs baselined (M6); override-honoured rate 100%; audit pass rate.
- **Gate:** none (prerequisite for R2 scale).
- **Risks:** council becomes a bottleneck → decision SLAs (e.g., 10 working days) and delegated thresholds.
- **Why not alternative:** a data-science-only model team would repeat the very silo the brief describes.

### R2 — Pilot-then-scale on unified data foundation (M4–24)
- **What:** identity/data foundation across Disney+/Hulu/ESPN in pilot markets with privacy-by-design; randomised holdout; 2 markets × 1–2 franchise clusters; scale waves after gate.
- **Owner:** CTO + Head of Product.
- **KPIs (define precisely):** (1) voluntary monthly churn Δ vs holdout (pre-registered minimum detectable effect set by data science; do not promise a figure); (2) **Discovery Breadth Index** = normalised Shannon entropy of a subscriber's viewing minutes across franchise clusters (must not fall; target rise); (3) cross-franchise adoption = % subscribers starting a title in a second franchise within 60 days; (4) guardrails: zero behavioural profiling on kids' profiles; exposure-diversity index.
- **Gate (M12):** significant churn/ breadth gain + all guardrails pass + creative-trust pulse ≥ threshold set in M0–3 + no unresolved regulatory issue → go/no-go. **Kill-switch:** any kids'-profile guardrail breach pauses rollout.
- **Cost shape:** three buckets — data & platform; people & training; governance & audit. Ranges to be validated in M0–3 (do not invent figures).
- **Evidence:** Gomez-Uribe & Hunt (2015); Davenport & Ronanki (2018); Vial (2019).
- **Risks:** integration slippage → phased scope; novelty effects → measure ≥ 90-day cohorts.
- **If pilot fails:** retain governance and data foundation (no-regret), revisit use cases (e.g., search and artwork only).

### R3 — Digital-confidence & hybrid-roles programme (M0–24)
- **What:** 3-tier academy; creative-technologist role family; champions network; shared balanced scorecard (engagement breadth + ecosystem value + trust + guardrails) used in creative and marketing reviews; incentives aligned (recognise experiments, not only hits).
- **Owner:** CHRO + Head of Creative Operations.
- **KPIs:** tier completion; trust pulse (quarterly, 1–5: "my judgement is respected", "I understand why recommendations are made"); % decisions citing test evidence; voluntary attrition among creatives (watch for negative signal).
- **Evidence:** Kotter (1995) as adapted; Hiatt (2006); Edmondson (1999); Orlikowski (1992).
- **Risks:** training seen as compliance → co-designed with creatives, sandbox first; change fatigue → sequence with pilot waves.

## 8. COUNTER-ARGUMENT & VIVA BANK (answer in 2–3 sentences; know your sources)
1. Why this technology over generative AI? → A1 scoring: fit, maturity, org-impact, ethical complexity.
2. How did you derive the churn figures? → lifetime = 1/churn; ARPU/churn assumptions sourced; illustrative; gross revenue.
3. Why trust Netflix's self-reported value? → I don't treat as proof; upper bound; pilot measures own effect.

5. Why only creative teams? → AC2 requires one; binding constraint on IP value; others covered in governance council.
6. Why Kotter if you criticise it? → useful scaffold; adapted as iterative loops; paired with ADKAR/Edmondson.
7. What is Goodhart's law and where does it apply? → target metrics get gamed; watch-time optimisation narrows content; hence balanced scorecard.
8. How do you measure discovery breadth? → normalised entropy across franchise clusters; guardrail.
9. What if the pilot fails? → keep no-regret assets; narrow use cases.
10. Does GDPR Art. 22 apply? → limited applicability; design principle; `[V]`.
11. Is the filter bubble real? → contested (Pariser vs Bruns); measure, don't assume.
12. How would you handle different regional regulations? → design to strictest standard; regional compliance partners in council.
13. Cost? → three buckets; ranges validated in M0–3; no invented figures.
14. Biggest weakness of your analysis? → illustrative model assumptions; limited Disney-internal data; mitigated by pilot.
15. What would change your recommendation? → failure at gate; regulatory change; evidence that veto slows delivery materially.
16. Where is the innovation? → creative veto + breadth KPI as shared objective; ecosystem-value orientation.
17. Why those owners? → map to accountability for IP, data, platform, people.
18. How does this fit Hulu/ESPN integration? → unified identity is the prerequisite and the data advantage.
19. Ethical trade-off? → possible short-term engagement cost accepted for trust.
