## 3. Improvements made beyond the blueprint

The blueprint contains conflicts and weak points. I resolved them as follows.

1. **Stale content removed.** The golden thread, 1.1 and the 3.1 "S1 to S3" link still referred to Mrs Okafor and care planning. They were rewritten for the hospital imaging case and its new S1 to S3. The thread's grammar ("builds that conditions") was also fixed.
2. **Pseudonym dropped.** The final case no longer needs "Mrs Okafor", and no patient name or pseudonym appears anywhere. The less patient-detail there is, the lower the identifiability risk.
3. **Reflective model critique strengthened.** The blueprint's 1.4 defends Gibbs, but 18.1 relies on Schön and tacit knowledge. I said so explicitly: Gibbs is the spine, and Schön covers the "just a feeling" moment that Gibbs handles poorly.
4. **Steel-man added to Issue 1.** The blueprint's old plan had a "case for the technology" block (Topol, 2019), but the revised 16.4 and 18.2 plans dropped it. I restored it, because the reader-reaction table asks for a counter-argument in each issue.
5. **Limitations rewritten.** The blueprint's limitation, "single simulated/taught case", no longer applies. It now reads: one real episode, from memory, outcome unknown, no empirical data.
6. **Article 22 handled carefully.** The blueprint says to check Art. 22 after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. I argued Art. 22 is unlikely to be engaged because a clinician reviews the output, and flagged that the current text must be checked.
7. **Original idea tied to the revised case.** S1 (concern-and-closure routine) is presented as the original, testable proposal, with a plan-do-study-act design and a measure. This also satisfies reading topic 6 (QI) in 2.5 and 3.3.
8. **Weakness of the proposal admitted.** S1 can be completed without being used, and counting notes rewards volume. The pilot therefore also asks whether a concern changed what happened next.
9. **Leadership paragraph added to 2.3.** It covers who sets the norm for questioning the tool (West, 2021; Healthcare Leadership Model). The blueprint's old 2.4c had this, but the revised plan lost it.
10. **Self-assessment critique made specific in 3.2.** It notes that the Care Certificate and Healthcare Leadership Model do not cover appraising AI tools, so that gap is measured against a self-set standard.
11. **Placeholder policy.** Wherever the blueprint says "[YOU SUPPLY]", I left a short, sized slot instead of inventing a fact. I made no claim about what you felt, said to your supervisor, or wrote down.
12. **Typo caught and fixed** in my own draft: "Nuclear Medicine and Midwifery Council" is now "Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC)".
13. **In-text citations and references.** Every in-text citation has a matching Harvard entry, and every entry is cited. I checked this with a script. Statutes are cited by section or regulation.
14. **Unsupported statements audited and referenced.** I went through every sentence and added a source wherever a factual claim had none:
    - Defensive practice under fear of blame (Berwick, 2013); the limits of Schön's model in writing (Finlay, 2008); hospital-based teamwork evidence (Reeves et al., 2017).
    - No AI-specific statute, and the clinical safety standards (MHRA, 2023; NHS England, 2018); power gradients and unheeded concerns (Francis, 2015).
    - The quality of SBAR evidence (Müller et al., 2018, new); plan-do-study-act (Langley et al., 2009, new); the anchoring explanation in 2.6 (Tversky and Kahneman, 1974; Parasuraman and Manzey, 2010).
    - UK GDPR articles named in text; NMC and Home Office pages for the career plan (new, both "no date" web sources to verify).
    - Some statements are my own reasoning or experience (for example, "nothing I know of requires..."). I deliberately left those uncited, worded as my own view, because inventing a source for them would be worse than none. A few were softened ("in the sources I read") where I could not support a general claim.
