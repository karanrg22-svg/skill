# 3. Career Development Plan

## 3.1 Destination and options appraisal

Within 12 months I aim to become a senior care assistant or team leader in the UK, within two to three years to take a quality or management step, and in the long term to contribute to services in Nepal. [CONFIRM that this is your goal.]

I appraised four options against fit with the Section 2 analysis, entry requirements, time and cost, and feasibility under visa rules. (i) Senior care assistant or team leader fits my existing experience and sits where handovers, induction and concern routes are set. (ii) Deputy or registered manager, through the Level 5 Diploma in Leadership and Management for Adult Care (Skills for Care, no date), has high fit but expects supervisory experience I lack. (iii) Postgraduate study in health management or policy would address the governance gap but costs more time and money. (iv) Nursing registration is attractive, but I must check Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) overseas requirements against my PCL qualification (Nursing and Midwifery Council, no date) and do not assume eligibility. I choose (i) because it is the earliest point at which I can apply S1 and S3 at team level. S2 needs organisational governance, so it belongs to later steps. The other options are deferred, not dismissed.

To present this identity to employers I will use a CV, supervisor references and a short professional profile. "Personal branding" language can reward self-presentation over practice, so I will evidence practice, such as feedback and a completed improvement cycle, not adjectives.

## 3.4 Risks, contingency, monitoring and long-term aim

The main risks are labour-market competition, cost and workload. Some routes depend on sponsorship, so I will check current Home Office rules (Home Office, no date) at each review, and my contingency is to prioritise routes that do not, such as part-time study alongside work. My view of demand rests on a national workforce report (Skills for Care, 2025), which describes sector-wide patterns and says little about vacancies in any one area, so I will check named employers directly before relying on it.

I will review progress every three months using my competency log, and change course if I hold no relevant interview after six months of applications or if feedback shows the supervisory gap persisting.

My long-term aim in Nepal is staged and evidence-informed. What should transfer is method: quality-improvement cycles, concern-and-closure routines and induction on new tools. What may not is UK statute and the regulator's structure, because Nepal's health-system context and resources differ (Ministry of Health and Population, 2023). I will treat contribution there as knowledge exchange before any larger role.

## 3.5 Closing line

Identity, case and plan describe one commitment: to notice, question and escalate, and, as responsibility grows, to build routines so that others can do the same, and so that a concern, once raised, is followed to an outcome that its author can learn from.
