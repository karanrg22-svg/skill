## 2. THE ARGUMENT (lock before opening PowerPoint)
| Element | Locked choice |
|---|---|
| **Technology (one)** | **Generative AI for localisation and accessibility**: LLM/neural machine translation plus speech technologies (transcription, text-to-speech, lip-sync), used in a **human-in-the-loop pipeline governed by autonomy levels** |
| **Problem** | **Global ambition outruns localisation capacity**: a premium catalogue must reach many languages quickly and affordably while cultural authenticity (a Disney strength) is at stake; localisation cost and lead time constrain library activation and day-and-date launches (linked to the brief's "balancing investment in original content with the costs of licensing and content production" and its international-markets focus) |
| **Stakeholder (one)** | **Localisation and regional-content teams** — in-house and vendor linguists, subtitlers, dubbing directors, regional content managers (the brief names "supporting employees across international markets" and allows "another of your choice") |
| **Thesis (S1–S2 and S11)** | GenAI creates **sustained** advantage in localisation only if **autonomy is earned tier by tier**: *scale* the library and long tail, *augment* mid-tier dubbing with consent, *protect* flagship and children's craft — because Disney's global differentiator is cultural authenticity, which the technology can't yet be trusted to guarantee |
| **Spine** | **ACTIVATE** (R1) → **AUGMENT** (R2) → **PROTECT** (R3) |
| **Three recommendations** | **R1** Activate: AI-assisted subtitling/metadata for the library and long tail · **R2** Augment: mid-tier dubbing with a Consent, Credit and Compensation Charter · **R3** Protect: a Craft Guarantee for flagship and children's content plus a talent partnership |
| **Thread phrases** | "scale the pipeline, protect the craft" · "AI drafts, people decide — where it matters" · "autonomy is earned, tier by tier" |

**Autonomy levels (state clearly that this is your adaptation of levels-of-automation research — Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens, 2000):**
- **L0 Human-led:** AI as a reference tool only (glossary lookup, QA checks).
- **L1 AI suggests:** AI proposes options; the human decides and produces.
- **L2 AI drafts, human edits:** post-editing with full human QA.
- **L3 AI produces, human audits:** sampled review with escalation rules.
- **L4 fully autonomous — not proposed;** a **red line** for creative/performance content.

**Five critical moves to land (these separate 70s from 80s):**
1. **Two problems, not one**: capacity *and* authenticity — a single technology must address both.
2. **Evidence-transfer caveat**: productivity studies measure other tasks, so pilots, tiers and gates are not optional.
3. **Autonomy follows the jagged frontier**: AI helps inside its capability frontier and hurts outside it.
4. **Treat resistance as identity defence** and design co-ownership (co-design council, psychological-contract renewal).
5. **Advantage lies in what Disney chooses *not* to automate** and in franchise language memory (glossaries, style guides, quality data), built with consent.
