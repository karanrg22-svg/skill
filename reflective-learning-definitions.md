# Reflective Learning: Two Clashing Definitions

## 1. Definition (150 words)

Kolb (1984) treats reflective learning as a cycle. You have a concrete experience, reflect on it, pull an abstract idea out of that reflection, then test the idea in practice, which hands you a new experience and starts the loop again. Reflection is one stage in that engine; skip a stage and the learning stalls.

Moon (1999) drops the stages. For her, reflective learning is what happens when a new experience gets worked into your existing cognitive structures — your assumptions, your prior understanding — and comes out the other side as a changed or better-integrated meaning. There's no required order and no fixed number of steps. Reflection can start anywhere and run at different depths, from a shallow description of what happened to a genuine rethink of what you believed.

So Kolb defines reflection by its shape. Moon defines it by what it does to you.

## 2. Kolb (1984) in my own words

Kolb builds a closed loop with a fixed shape. Something happens to you (concrete experience). You notice what happened and how (reflective observation). You pull a rule or principle out of it (abstract conceptualisation). You go test that rule in a new situation (active experimentation), which gives you your next experience, and the cycle restarts. Reflection is the hinge between raw experience and abstract theory, not the whole of learning. Kolb's claim is basically procedural: work through the stages in roughly this order and learning follows. The process is the theory.

## 3. Moon (1999) instead

Moon cares less about which stages you pass through and more about what actually changes in your head. Reflective learning, for her, is taking a messy, unstructured experience and working it into what you already believe: comparing it against your existing framework, noticing where it doesn't fit, and either adjusting the new material to fit or reorganising the framework itself. No named sequence. Reflection can be triggered at any point and can be shallow or deep. What matters is whether your understanding was actually restructured, or whether you just described the experience back to yourself. The outcome is the theory.

## 4. Where they actually disagree

The real fault line is process versus outcome. Kolb tells you reflection happened because you completed the cycle. Moon tells you it happened because your understanding changed, however you got there. That difference bites in practice: someone can grind through all four Kolb stages and produce nothing but a restatement of events — box ticked, nothing learned. By Moon's standard, that person never reflected at all, even though their process looked correct. Flip it around: someone can jump straight from an experience to a changed belief with no orderly stages, which Moon counts as reflective learning without hesitation, but which doesn't map onto Kolb's sequence at all. The two theories disagree about what evidence even counts — finishing a procedure, or showing that meaning actually shifted.
