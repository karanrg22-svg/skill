## A. Central argument (the thread a harsh marker must see)

**Thesis (use in Section 1, prove in Section 2):**
Travelodge's lean, low-cost hotel model is commercially efficient, but it generates structural people-management tensions. Minimum-wage-anchored pay caps what reward can do for motivation and retention. Standardised, thinly staffed roles intensify workload and emotional labour. High churn then erodes training investment. These problems come from the model and the labour market, not from individual managers.

**The "so what" loop (Section 2 synthesis):** turnover raises the training burden, which raises workload on remaining staff, which raises burnout and exits, which lowers service consistency.

**The critical hook (use it, it is the strongest evidence in the file):** vendor case studies report that Travelodge engagement scores rose for five years while attrition also rose (Hive HR, 2019). If true, engagement alone did not retain staff. That supports the argument that retention is driven by structural factors (pay, labour market, job design) as much as by engagement. Flag the source as vendor-produced and self-reported, and say so in one clause. That candour is itself marked up.

### Synthesis (~30 words)
Show the loop: low reward leads to churn, churn raises training load, training load adds pressure on remaining staff, pressure leads to burnout and exit. State the performance cost (service consistency, recruitment spend). Do not recommend anything. One sentence may point forward ("Section 3 evaluates whether current practice breaks this cycle").

## G. Drafting protocol
1. Draft each block with tags [T]/[S] in the margin, then convert to in-text wording ("At Travelodge...", "Across UK hospitality...") and delete the tags.
2. Max two citations per sentence. Every paragraph has at least one academic and one empirical source.
3. Name a theory once, then use its logic. No definitions.
4. End each challenge with a one-sentence consequence for employees AND performance.
5. No hedging ("may perhaps"). Make the claim, show the evidence, qualify only where the evidence is weak.
6. Keep the tone of an analyst writing to a hotel operations director: precise, impersonal, no "I", no rhetorical questions.
7. Running count after each block. Target 600 words for Sections 1 and 2 combined.
8. Final pass: remove every adjective that carries no evidence; check each number has a date and a source; check that nothing in Section 2 is a recommendation.
